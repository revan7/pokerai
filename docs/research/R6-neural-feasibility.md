# R6 — Training a neural poker agent versus a solver-based assistant

Research cutoff: **2026-09-10**. Target supplied by the user: Windows 11, manually entered live 6-max NLHE cash states, action frequencies and EV in approximately 5–15 seconds; RTX 5080 16 GB, i7-13700K, 64 GB RAM; baseline in 1–2 weeks.

**Recommendation: solver-based phase 1; optional, narrowly scoped neural training in phase 2.** Start with precomputed six-seat preflop strategies and a range-conditioned heads-up postflop solver. Use solver-generated labels later to train a small policy/action-value surrogate, then consider a DeepStack-style counterfactual-value leaf model. Do not make a newly trained whole-game six-player agent a delivery dependency. This is an engineering judgment based on the evidence below, not a measured result on the target machine.

**Critical scope limit:** a six-seat table and a postflop pot with three or more active players are different requirements. The practical postflop engines examined solve heads-up trees; an assistant built around them does not thereby solve arbitrary multiway cash poker. GTOpen explicitly distinguishes its heads-up postflop solver from its multiway preflop continuation approximation. [GTOpen scope](https://github.com/MatthewPDingle/GTOpen)

## 1. Evidence standard and interpretation

This was a read-only paper/repository review. No source was executed, installed, compiled, or benchmarked; only this report was created. “Source available” means inspectable implementation and documented workflow, not that it ran successfully here. **Working on this exact Windows/Python/Node/GPU configuration: not verified** for every candidate. Maintenance dates below mean the newest default-branch commit visible in the cited history at review time; they are not promises of support. Missing licenses, weights, compute details, or independent evaluations are explicitly marked **not verified**.

“Full NLHE” below means ordinary hold'em cards and all betting streets, even where an agent restricts its bet menu or abstracts hands. It does **not** mean exact solution of every legal wager. “HU” means two players; “FHP” is a shortened limit hold'em research game. Deep CFR's FHP/HULH experiments and SD-CFR's five-card-flop variant must not be presented as six-player no-limit results. [Deep CFR, §5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/brown19b/brown19b.pdf), [SD-CFR, §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.07621)

For reported win rates, **1 mbb/hand = 0.1 bb/100** by unit conversion. Preserve each paper's uncertainty convention: standard error, standard deviation, and 95% confidence intervals are not interchangeable. Different opponents, bet menus, stacks, variance reduction, and evaluation protocols prevent a simple cross-paper ranking. A Slumbot heads-up result is not evidence of six-player strength. [ReBeL evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13544), [Student of Games evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03178)

## 5. Feasibility on the specified workstation

### 5.1 Compute arithmetic does not establish reproducibility

Assuming continuous operation, **14 days = 336 GPU-hours** on one GPU and **60 days = 1,440 GPU-hours**. Multiplying by the supplied 16 physical cores gives 5,376 and 23,040 nominal core-hours. These are arithmetic capacity ceilings, not training forecasts: development, evaluation, CPU/GPU contention, memory limits, and heterogeneous core throughput reduce useful work. A 2026 consumer GPU-hour cannot be converted directly into a V100, TPU, or older CPU-hour without benchmarking; such conversion is **not verified**.

AlphaHoldem's published eight-GPU/64-core run is evidence against declaring all HUNL neural learning impossible on consumer hardware. It is not evidence that its unpublished complete pipeline will scale cleanly to one 16-GB GPU or to six players. ReBeL's 720-GPU data-generation setup and DeepStack's large solve-label budget show why quoting only final network-fitting time is misleading. **Inference:** training a modest network can be affordable while generating strategically valid labels or self-play remains the bottleneck. [AlphaHoldem](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/20394), [ReBeL appendix](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13544), [DeepStack supplement](https://johanson.ca/publications/poker/2017-science-deepstack/2017-science-deepstack-supplement.pdf)

Missing private information, selected teachers, and domain shift remain. [PokerGPT method](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06781), [PHH dataset](https://github.com/uoftcprg/phh-dataset) |

The material engineering risk for old neural repositories is portability as well as computation: PokerRL-era Python/PyTorch, DeepStack Lua/Torch, and PyStack TensorFlow 1.13 are not documented as the supplied modern stack. A native Rust/C++ solver boundary makes a narrower integration target, but its exact build on this machine is still **not verified**. This is a scheduling judgment based on declared dependencies. [PokerRL](https://github.com/EricSteinberger/PokerRL), [DeepStack prerequisites](https://github.com/lifrordi/DeepStack-Leduc), [PyStack](https://github.com/doas3140/PyStack), [Desktop Postflop](https://github.com/b-inary/desktop-postflop)

## 6. Decision quality, latency, and risk versus solver-based play

### 6.1 What the solver can and cannot establish

For **two active players**, a postflop solve can calculate a strategy and action values conditional on both players' starting ranges, the board, pot/stacks, rake settings, and available bets. That is directly aligned with the requested output. It still solves the supplied model: a small exploitability value does not certify that the entered ranges or bet tree represent the live opponents correctly. GTOpen documents this conditional scope; the b-inary library exposes strategy/value-oriented postflop functionality. [GTOpen result interpretation](https://github.com/MatthewPDingle/GTOpen), [postflop library documentation](https://b-inary.github.io/postflop_solver/postflop_solver/)

For **three or more active players**, do not combine pairwise HU solutions and call the result a multiway equilibrium. Additional players change ranges, action incentives, continuation policies, and pot eligibility. Pluribus itself notes the absence of the two-player zero-sum convergence guarantee in multiplayer poker and uses a distinct multiplayer search design. Also, action-dependent rake makes player payoffs non-zero-sum; merely enabling rake in a solver does not restore the standard zero-sum theorem. [Pluribus theory and search](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aay2400), [CFR foundations](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2007/hash/08d98638c6fcd194a4b1e6992063e944-Abstract.html)

Recommended range handling is a design requirement, not a claim of existing product capability: start from six-seat preflop strategies and update ranges using the **entire observed action sequence**, including sizes. Maintain hero's strategic range, then select the actual hero combination for display; treating hero's known cards as a publicly known singleton range changes the game being solved. Carry card removal and, where supported, folded-player information into the HU subgame. The b-inary solver's support for up to four folded players' bunching does **not** mean four additional active opponents. [ReBeL public-belief formulation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13544), [b-inary bunching support](https://github.com/b-inary/postflop-solver)

Recommended EV contract: report each legal candidate's expected continuation payoff in bb under an explicit model, alongside its strategy frequency. Normalize the solver's counterfactual values correctly before presenting actual-hand EV. State whether values are incremental from this decision; with that convention, folding has zero future payoff and prior chips are sunk. Regret predictions, action probabilities, equity, and realized hand winnings are different quantities; a policy-only network does not supply action EV. These are implementation requirements derived from the distinct quantities in regret learning and resolving. [Deep CFR definitions](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/brown19b/brown19b.pdf), [DeepStack formulation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724)

| Dimension | Own whole-game neural agent | Preflop library + HU postflop solver | Solver labels + compact neural surrogate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality within a covered HU state | Depends on training, coverage, and approximation; sparse states can be weak despite plausible actions. | Directly inspectable frequencies/EV and convergence within its configured tree. | Can approximate teacher outputs; introduces held-out prediction error. |
| Six-player/multiway scope | Can be encoded, but training support does not establish strength. | HU backend does not solve three-plus-player postflop pots. | Does not acquire multiway competence from HU labels. |
| Frequency semantics | Must recover the algorithm's actual average/mixed strategy; a generic classifier's confidence is insufficient. | Explicit mixed policy for the solved model. | Must train on mixed-policy targets rather than one observed action. |
| EV output | Needs an action-value head, calibrated rollouts, or search; policy alone is insufficient. | Available from the solution, with correct normalization/conventions. | Needs value labels for all candidate actions and uncertainty validation. |
| Latency | Forward pass can be fast; belief updates and search may dominate. | Variable with tree/ranges/accuracy; caching and smaller trees are practical controls. | Fast lookup-like inference is plausible; exact target-machine latency **not verified**. |
| Delivery risk | High: rules, sampling, training, values, evaluation, portability. | Lower for bounded HU coverage; high if presented as an arbitrary six-max solver. | Moderate incremental risk after a validated teacher exists. |

The comparison is engineering synthesis, not a measured contest between implementations. Its technical basis is the separation between regret/value/policy models and search in the original systems, and the explicit scope of the available solvers. [SD-CFR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.07621), [DeepStack-Leduc algorithm explanation](https://github.com/lifrordi/DeepStack-Leduc), [postflop solver](https://github.com/b-inary/postflop-solver)

**Concrete GPU timing evidence:** GTOpen's 2026-09-07 report used Windows, RTX 3090 24 GB, Ryzen 5950X, 64 GB RAM, 16 solver threads. It reports 11.107 seconds for a rainbow flop and 6.667 seconds for a two-tone flop at its unchanged 0.3%-pot target. These measurements **exclude initial tree construction and GPU upload**. ## 7. Supervised learning from human hand histories

**Imitation alone cannot establish a profit-maximizing assistant.** Its objective is to predict the observed teacher's action, not to evaluate all available actions' counterfactual expected returns. A good imitation model can reproduce human errors or a strategy specialized to the historical opponent pool. PokerGPT explicitly trains on selected human behavior, uses player win rates as reward-model labels, and evaluates multiplayer play against rule bots; this is insufficient evidence of profit maximization against arbitrary live six-max opponents. This conclusion concerns the objective and evidence, not a claim that imitation can never yield a winning player. [PokerGPT methodology/evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06781)

### Available datasets and what their labels mean

| Dataset | Verified contents / access | Appropriate use and limitations |
|---|---|---|

| **Pluribus evaluation histories** — [PHH benchmark documentation](https://phh.readthedocs.io/en/stable/benchmark.html) | 10,000 six-player NLHE histories from the professional evaluation. | Useful six-player action/rules replay material. Recommended safeguards for any imitation experiment are methodological judgments: split by player/session/time rather than randomly splitting actions from the same hand; exclude future board/results from model inputs; distinguish observed private cards from hidden/mucked cards; and audit selection bias. Showdown-only filtering systematically omits many folds and successful non-showdown bluffs; selecting players by noisy historical winnings does not provide ground-truth values for each of their actions. The documented filtering and corpus provenance make these concerns concrete. [PokerGPT showdown filter](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06781), [PHH source inventory](https://github.com/uoftcprg/phh-dataset)

Recommended role of human data: **opponent priors and behavioral prediction**, with cautious adaptation from newly observed actions; optional initialization of a policy model. To pursue higher expected profit against a modeled population, combine those priors with value computation/search and evaluate the resulting policy under the actual rake/stack rules. Outcome regression or offline RL may help, but observed histories alone do not identify the values of every unobserved action. Havoc is relevant research in this direction; its reproducible six-max strength remains **not verified** here. [Havoc publication record](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/team-of-rivals-hierarchical-deep-reinforcement-learning-and-behav/), [DeepStack value/search separation](https://github.com/lifrordi/DeepStack-Leduc)

## 8. Recommended delivery sequence and acceptance criteria

The following is a proposed implementation plan, not work performed during this research task.

### Phase 1: weeks 1–2 — solver-based baseline

1. **Define and validate the state contract.** Include positions, full action history, contributions, stacks, legal raise/all-in handling, board/cards, pot, and rake/cap rules. Derive ranges from a specified preflop source. Preflop charts must match seat count, stack depth, rake, and available sizing assumptions; limps/straddles/unequal stacks need explicitly supported coverage or a visible fallback. A particular preflop library's completeness and licensing were not selected or verified by R6.
2. **Integrate a source-auditable HU postflop engine**, with b-inary's Rust solver the first reference candidate and TexasSolver an alternative. Accept DCFR where practical instead of requiring the literal CFR+ variant; compare resulting accuracy and latency. Existing implementation details and licenses are in §6.2. [b-inary solver](https://github.com/b-inary/postflop-solver), [TexasSolver](https://github.com/bupticybee/TexasSolver)
3. **Return real mixed strategies and action EV**, with explicit range/model assumptions. Distinguish solved HU outputs from multiway approximations. A baseline that labels unresolved three-plus-player postflop states honestly is preferable to claiming verified six-max equilibrium coverage. This is a product scope recommendation based on the engines' documented boundaries. [GTOpen scope](https://github.com/MatthewPDingle/GTOpen)
4. **Cache a small set of common flop configurations and solve turn/river states as needed.** Measure complete request latency before expanding library breadth. Five to fifteen seconds is an acceptance target, **not verified performance**; full flop coverage under all range/stack/bet configurations is not implied by a board library. [GPU benchmark and setup costs](https://github.com/MatthewPDingle/GTOpen/blob/master/research/autoresearch/gpu-pass.md)
5. **GPU role: optional batch pre-solving, no mandatory neural training.** With the recommended CPU engine alone, the RTX 5080 has **no solver role**. If a GPU backend passes license, correctness, memory, and speed checks early, use it first for overnight/batch HU flop solves and label generation; consider online use only after end-to-end measurements. GTOpen is a source-visible evaluation lead, not yet a verified licensed dependency; TexasSolverGPU is private-source distribution. Do not spend the baseline schedule writing a new CUDA CFR engine. [GTOpen](https://github.com/MatthewPDingle/GTOpen), [TexasSolverGPU notice](https://github.com/bupticybee/TexasSolverGPU/blob/main/EULA.md)

### Phase 2: weeks 3–8 — bounded supervised neural acceleration

**Preferred approach:** train a compact, structured model on **our validated solver's full policy and action-value outputs**, initially for a restricted set of HU turn/river or common flop configurations. Inputs should encode board/hand, both players' ranges or a validated range representation, pot/stack, betting history, legal action menu, and relevant rules. Targets should retain mixed frequencies and values of all candidate actions. This is a scoped engineering proposal motivated by solver-label learning; no specific model size, training duration, or accuracy on this workstation is yet verified. [PokerBench solver-supervision precedent](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08328), [DeepStack supervised values](https://johanson.ca/publications/poker/2017-science-deepstack/2017-science-deepstack-supplement.pdf)

Use the model first to compress/approximate a library or warm-start recommendations, with an exact-solver fallback for uncovered or uncertain states. If that demonstrably improves latency without material EV loss, progress to a **DeepStack-inspired range-conditioned counterfactual-value network for depth-limited HU resolving**. That second step needs correct resolving semantics and separate validation of leaf error; a scalar hand-equity predictor is not a substitute. Keep whole-game ReBeL training, six-player SD-CFR/DREAM, and Pluribus reproduction on a research track outside the product dependency chain. [DeepStack resolving](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724), [SD-CFR strategy representation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.07621)

Recommended acceptance criteria before relying on a new backend/model:

- **Rules and accounting:** compare hand transitions, legal actions, chip conservation, payouts, card removal, and side-pot behavior against an independent reference where applicable; the public Pluribus histories can supply replay fixtures, not a strategic oracle. [PHH benchmark](https://phh.readthedocs.io/en/stable/benchmark.html)
- **Decision quality:** compare held-out states with substantially longer reference solves; measure action EV loss and tail errors, not just matching the most frequent action. Include unseen boards, ranges, stack depths, and off-menu bet sizes. Solver-conditioned quality and model generalization must be reported separately. [PokerBench evaluation scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.08328), [postflop solver](https://github.com/b-inary/postflop-solver)
- **Playing strength:** use independent opponent mixtures, seat rotation, duplicate-deal/variance-reduction protocols where appropriate, sufficient samples, and confidence intervals in bb/100 after the intended rake. A HU benchmark may validate a HU module; it must not be relabelled a six-player win rate. Proposed local results: **not verified**. [Libratus evaluation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29249696/), [DeepStack evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724)
- **Latency and reliability:** measure median and p95 complete requests, cold and warm starts, peak RAM/VRAM, timeout/fallback frequency, and numerical agreement across CPU/GPU precision modes. Require the intended 5–15-second behavior on representative states before expanding scope. The existing GPU timing exclusions explain why iteration or forward-pass timings are insufficient. [GTOpen timing methodology](https://github.com/MatthewPDingle/GTOpen/blob/master/research/autoresearch/gpu-pass.md)

**Decision:** phase 1 should produce an auditable solver-backed assistant with a clearly bounded HU postflop scope. Phase 2 should use neural training only where measured solver latency or library size warrants it. **Skip end-to-end six-max neural self-play as a product prerequisite; do not claim profit-maximizing human imitation or a verified local cash win rate.** These are recommendations from this evidence review, not experimentally established outcomes on the supplied hardware.
